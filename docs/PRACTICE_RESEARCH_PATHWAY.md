# Practice-Research Pathway

## Proposed research area

**Working title:** *Narrative Governance: Leadership, Authorship, and External
Working Memory in Founder-Led Independent Game Studios*

The project asks how leadership and production structures in small, founder-led
studios shape—and are shaped by—the narrative systems they build. The creator's
longitudinal archive is the primary practice case, supported by comparative cases
from other independent studios.

## Candidate research questions

1. How can a long-running transmedia archive be converted into an auditable game-IP
   system without erasing its associative and nonlinear creative logic?
2. How do disability, neurodivergence, and external working-memory tools affect
   authorship, creative control, and studio governance?
3. How can public-domain material and cultural inheritance be used critically while
   preserving attribution and distinguishing legal availability from ethical use?
4. Which governance practices let collaborators contribute real perspectives while
   retaining consent, credit, and clear ownership boundaries?
5. How can mechanics make domination, leadership, restraint, and repair observable
   as different player behaviours?

## Method

- Practice research through iterative game and narrative prototypes
- Archival analysis of dated manuscripts, decks, repositories, and design decisions
- Reflective documentation of tool-assisted synthesis and production
- Comparative case studies of independent game studios
- Playtesting focused on player interpretation and leadership behaviour
- Optional interviews only after institutional ethics approval and consent

## Expected contribution

The research would contribute a documented method for converting nonlinear creative
archives into governable transmedia systems; a framework for distinguishing archive,
influence, canon, and rules authority; and a practice account of AI as disclosed
external working memory rather than retroactive authorship.

## Pre-existing IP boundary

Before any application or institutional access, create a dated schedule listing all
pre-existing manuscripts, characters, names, art, code, repositories, mechanics,
brands, timelines, and business materials. Academic study may analyse selected
artifacts without assigning the underlying commercial ecosystem to an institution.

Any university agreement must be reviewed for:

- ownership of pre-existing and newly created IP;
- obligations to assign research-program IP;
- publication, repository-deposit, and open-access requirements;
- confidentiality and embargo options;
- revenue sharing and commercialisation;
- treatment of software, datasets, participant material, and AI-assisted work.

## Staged institutional engagement

### Stage 1 — Fit conversation

Provide a two-page concept, public repository links, CV, and a one-page development
timeline. Ask about supervisory fit, practice-research methods, part-time and remote
attendance, international fees, funding, and institutional IP terms.

### Stage 2 — Limited review

Provide a read-only, curated evidence index. Do not provide credentials, benefit or
housing records, private correspondence, raw participant information, or the complete
unpublished archive.

### Stage 3 — Application

Submit the institution's requested proposal and academic records only after the
supervisor, delivery model, funding plan, accessibility support, and IP boundary have
been confirmed in writing.

### Stage 4 — Research governance

Use ethics approval, contributor consent, versioned decision records, and separate
academic and commercial workstreams throughout the project.

## Decision gate

Doctoral enrollment is justified only if it provides material value that independent
development cannot provide as efficiently: qualified supervision, defensible research
method, peer evaluation, institutional access, publication support, or funding.
