# Battle the Beast — Wattpad Archive Review and Practice Findings

## Scope and status

This is a retrospective of the seven Battle the Beast Wattpad works supplied by the author, with their displayed chapter titles, dates and word counts. It maps the published archive against the contest’s stated prompt and the television material available around Season 2. It evaluates the work as a fan-writing exercise and records what it demonstrates for the author’s current original projects.

This is not a rights opinion, a revival pitch, or a claim that the submitted versions are recovered. The original contest rules page is no longer live. The prompt, 1,500-word cap, 39-prior-loops premise, tag, judges, prize, and closing window are reported in archived listings and the author’s historical notes; they have not all been verified against a primary rules copy in this review.

The full Wattpad text and the chapter metadata below were supplied by the author from Wattpad’s story editor and retained in the private author archive. The public repository does not reproduce those texts, embedded songs, or images.

## Executive finding

The strongest contest fit is the idea of examining a discarded attempt against the Beast: the premise expressly invited writers into the story’s unseen time-loop space. The strongest craft discovery is the relationship between Luna’s time navigation and Isadore’s power brokering, including the costs of using power alone and the different ways power affects them. The visual sequence in “WTF” also carries an intelligible progression from divided identity, through authority and the burden of repair, to reciprocal power and an uncontrolled release.

The available Wattpad publication record does **not** establish that the seven complete works were the contest submissions. All supplied chapter publication dates fall after the reported February 2017 close; several works were published in installments over later years. Six of the seven present-day work totals exceed the reported 1,500-word limit. Earlier submitted drafts or revisions may have differed, but no contemporaneous submitted copies or revision history have been supplied. Therefore this review treats them as the published archive and does not certify contest eligibility.

At the time the contest closed, Season 2 had begun airing. SYFY lists “Knight of Crowns” (S2E1) as aired January 25, 2017, and its episode recap places the group in the aftermath of the Beast confrontation. “Hotel Spa Potions” (S2E2) aired February 1. “Divine Elimination” (S2E3) aired February 8, the reported contest close date; whether it had aired before the submission deadline depends on the missing rules’ exact cutoff time and time zone. The contest’s prior-loop premise offered a legitimate speculative gap, but the show’s already-established character identities and outcomes remained boundaries if a story were meant to fit television continuity.

**Classification:** these are valuable historical fan-work experiments. They are not a lost season outline or a canon-compatible continuation package. Read as stories set in discarded or divergent timelines, they can explore possibility; read as the television series’ actual explanation of events, several introduce alternate origins and relationships that the aired series does not establish.

## What the work got right

### 1. It used the prompt’s speculative space

A story about one discarded loop is a natural fit for the “previous attempts” invitation. The archive repeatedly treats time as lived experience rather than a clean reset: Penny remembers, Luna navigates through music, Fogg carries the burden of repeated attempts, and the group’s choices leave emotional residue. That is a strong response to the premise and an effective interactive-story engine.

### 2. It saw power as relational, costly, and ethically unstable

Luna’s music opens or guides movement through time; Isadore can broker, amplify, bind, or destroy power. Quinton grounds or catalyzes other people’s abilities, while Julia tries to bind distributed abilities into a whole. Their powers do not simply stack: connection can stabilize, misdirect, overwhelm, or make a person dependent. This is the archive’s clearest transferable craft insight.

The cost is not only physical. The stories ask who gets to choose, who remembers, who is made into a weapon, and whether saving someone can become control. Penny’s refusal and Isadore’s failure to honor it expose a meaningful tension between care and agency. That tension is stronger when dramatized through a specific choice than when explained in a long cosmological monologue.

### 3. The image sequence in “The Beast” works as a visual argument

The supplied sequence moves through five distinct functions:

1. A three-face composite suggests identity split across incompatible versions.
2. Fogg’s raised hand and marked palm place institutional authority and magical responsibility in one image.
3. Julia’s line about fixing “big things” makes repair feel consequential rather than effortless.
4. Blue and red hands touching make power exchange visible as a relationship with two active sides.
5. The cosmic explosion image ends on consequence at a scale no single character can comfortably contain.

This is useful visual thinking: each image changes how the reader understands the next beat. The imagery does not by itself establish a clear causal rule or a canon fact. Its assets appear to include third-party television imagery and a watermarked wallpaper; none should be reused in an original commercial project without separate rights review.

### 4. It found a bridge from individual events to inherited consequence

“The Truth About Your Birth” connects a present mother, her mother, and a future child through a shared time-related gift and an attempt to mend a generational wound. That is the clearest formal bridge from a single magical crisis to the lineage structure of Urban Alien Adventures. It is a structural and thematic lesson, not a transferable franchise plot.

## Where development was needed

These are observations about the work’s 2017–2019 development, not instructions to revise the preserved archive.

| Development issue | Where it appears | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| The causal rules keep expanding | “The Beast,” “My name is Luna,” “SoulCaliber,” and “A to Z of Magic” add soul splitting, energy transfer, a sixth finger, corporeal birth, music-based time travel, memory replacement, and 40 resets. | Readers need a stable rule and cost before a new exception can feel earned. The archive’s strongest ideas risk becoming harder to evaluate when every consequence introduces another mechanism. |
| Names and identity layers are hard to track | Quinton/Quentin, Aster/Izadore/Izzy/Nova, Alice/Aster, Luna as sister/daughter, and multiple versions of Julia recur across stories. | A reader unfamiliar with the author’s private cosmology can lose track of who is present, which timeline is active, and what each relationship means. Short orientation cues and a clear reveal order would have helped. |
| Multiple arcs compete for the same short-story space | The stories move among the Beast, Julia’s godhood, Penny and Kady, Luna’s birth, Fogg’s origin, the keys, reincarnation, and timeline ethics. | Each installment needs a dominant character decision and a consequence. The material currently behaves more like a linked serial mythology than a self-contained 1,500-word contest scene. |
| Some revelations are explained instead of staged | Several passages pause for extended metaphysics, exposition, song references, or direct interpretation of the moral. | The underlying ideas are strong; readers can infer more when powers and limits are demonstrated through an action, a failed attempt, and a choice. |
| Trauma becomes a mechanism for cosmology | “The Beast,” “Straight Outta Filory,” and related origin passages connect sexual violence to power transfer, Martin’s transformation, and Luna/Isadore’s altered existence. | The subject needs deliberate control of point of view, aftermath, consent, and character agency. Its gravity can be obscured when the assault chiefly explains a magical origin or plot mechanism. This report records the issue without rewriting that authorial decision. |
| Tone and naming shift rapidly | Comedy, pop-culture references, cosmic tragedy, romantic destiny, explicit violence, and satirical asides sit close together. | Tonal shifts can be intentional, but transitions and viewpoint need to signal whether a moment is comic, tragic, or analytical. |

## What carries forward to current original projects

Keep the craft findings separate from *The Magicians* material:

- **The RIM Experience:** strongest foundation for why the tales are being told—ownership, consciousness, belonging, and who has authority to define or claim a person.
- **The Legend of Lady Violet and Sebastian:** use the existing material to investigate how inherited power limits freedom and agency, including when someone believes they are acting protectively.
- **See You When I Get There:** retain the mother/self/future-child structure as a conversation across time about a shared gift and a generational wound.
- **Urban Alien Adventures:** keep these as a lineage of already-existing works. Do not add a new world, mythology, or franchise-derived character to make the connection appear stronger.
- **Play It Forward:** use the practical development lesson—how recurring community participation, accessible activities, and post-event connection can sustain interest while the story’s decisions remain author-led. This is a production and community method, not a new story arc.
- **Veiled Dominion and Duet:** carry only franchise-neutral questions for testing: what makes power costly, how interdependence changes action, whether a player can refuse a harmful “solution,” and whether repair preserves agency.

Do not transfer The Magicians characters, names, settings, plot, magic rules, dialogue, music, or images. Treat *The Nines*, *Altered Carbon*, *Warrior Nun*, *Black Mirror*, and *Kaleidoscope* as author-stated formal or tonal influences, not source canon and not a mandate to copy their story elements.
